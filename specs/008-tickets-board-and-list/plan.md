# Plan: Tickets board and list

Spec: `specs/008-tickets-board-and-list/spec.md`

## Order of work

Everything 008 reads sits on 007's server half, which is on `main`: the Jira client, each connection's working credentials, `cache.db` and the sync with its schedule. The sprint becomes a third part of that same sync, next to Jira's assigned issues and GitHub, so Tickets keeps the same 2-minute rhythm, Refresh and Clear cache without a second scheduler.

1. **Contracts first** (task 1). `GET /api/tickets` answers with the sprint (name, end, project key), its tickets and where Jira's sprint read stands. The state reuses 007's `InboxToolStateSchema`, so Tickets says a failed sync in 007's words (Q8d, Q9j). Each ticket carries data, never words that change with the clock: the due date as a calendar day, the priority's own name and its bar level, the column, the assignee's name and whether it's you, and the session working on it. The app writes "Due Tue" and "Overdue · 2 d" with the laptop's calendar, as Home does. No new event: the sync already ends with `inbox.changed`, which becomes "a sync ended" for Tickets too.
2. **The reads** (tasks 2 and 3), in Jira's `client.ts`, the only file that talks to Jira:
   - who you are (`/myself`, for "Assigned to you"), the sprint field (found in `/rest/api/3/field` by its type `com.pyxis.greenhopper.jira:gh-sprint`, never its name), and your issues in open sprints with that field (`assignee = currentUser() AND sprint in openSprints()`). That gives each active sprint you're in, with its board.
   - the board's configuration (`/rest/agile/1.0/board/{id}/configuration`: its columns with their status ids; a board that's gone or hidden is no columns, not a problem), and every issue in the chosen sprint (`sprint = <id> ORDER BY Rank ASC`) through `search/jql`'s `nextPageToken`, 100 a page, at most 300, with whether more were left.
   - the priority order, which 007's client already reads.
3. **The map** (task 4). A pure `sprint-map.ts`: pick the sprint (Q1), take the page bar's project key from the sprint's issue keys (one project gives its key, several give none, Q9a), put each status in a column by category and the board's column order (Q2), give each priority its bars (Q7a), and turn issues into cache records with titles and metadata only.
4. **The tickets service** (tasks 5 and 6). It syncs the sprint into `cache.db` as its own part (`jira-sprint`), with its own last sync and problem, so a failed sprint read never marks Home's Jira rows as failed. It reads it back with the session store joined: the newest session on each ticket, unless it's Stopped (Q4c). Then the inbox's `run()` syncs it side by side with the other two. A disconnect and Clear cache take it with them, and so does a sync that finds Jira disconnected. The cache's tables don't change, so its version stays at 1. The spec's assumption expected a bump only for a table change.
5. **The route** (task 7). `GET /api/tickets` is wired into `createServer`. The leak test also searches the sprint part for tokens and description text (AC-28).
6. **api-client and live updates** (task 8). `tickets()` and its query options. `inbox.changed` (a sync ended), `status.changed` (Jira connected or disconnected) and `sessions.changed` (the agent chip) refresh it.
7. **The words and the filters** (tasks 9 and 10), as pure functions with a fixed clock. Home's calendar helpers move to one shared file, so both screens write dates the same way.
8. **The `@devhub/ui` parts** (tasks 11 to 13): `SearchField` with its 32 px size, `Board` and `BoardColumn` with a loading form, and `Table` from shadcn's table. Each gets stories and tests, and its design-map row.
9. **The screen** (tasks 14 to 18): page bar and toolbar with the filters in the address, the board, the list, the states, focus during a sync and the narrow layout.
10. **The sidebar count, docs, then the real browser** (tasks 19 to 21). Then `/review`.
