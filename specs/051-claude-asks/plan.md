# Plan: Claude asks: a question in the session, answered there

Spec: `specs/051-claude-asks/spec.md`

## Order of work

1. **The `question` approval kind, end to end in types.** Add it to `SessionApprovalSchema`, to the waiting and denied steps, to the approval log and to the notification's `about`. Add an `answered` step and an answer in the approval request's body. Adding a kind to these unions breaks every place that switches over them, so the same commit gives each one its words (Q3), as spec 044's `jira` kind did in its Task 6. Typecheck then stays green from the first commit.
2. **Core, from the call inward.** `tool-decision.ts` turns `AskUserQuestion` into a `question` ask, or refuses a malformed one. `claude-adapter.ts` answers the parked call: `answers` go into `updatedInput`, Skip gets the skip line, and Stop interrupts. `session-service.ts` and the route check the answer against the waiting question before anything reaches Claude. Each layer is tested on its own: the decision as a pure function, the adapter over `test/fake-claude-sdk.ts`, and the route with `app.inject()`.
3. **The brief's line and the notification.** These are small and live in core only. They come after the flow works, so the brief never tells Claude to use a tool it can't use yet.
4. **api-client.** `postApproval` sends the answers, parsed with the contract.
5. **`@devhub/ui`: `ChoiceCard` with `multiple`** (Q2a). The app needs it, and it stands alone with a story and a test.
6. **App: the question in the callout.** First the form (groups, Other, Send answer, validation), then Skip, a failed send, focus and the pinned bar. After that come the words around it: the step, the pill, Home's banner, the notifications and Settings › Notifications' row.
7. **e2e.** The fake Claude Code learns "(asks)" and plays a real `AskUserQuestion` over the SDK's protocol. One e2e file proves the answer reaches Claude, that Skip works, and that both themes have no axe violations.
8. **Docs.** `learn/sessions-and-worktrees.md` and the agent-runtime skill say that questions exist.
